Your passwords, in a vault only you can open

Orchard Password Manager keeps your logins in one encrypted file on your own computer. It locks itself when you step away, takes copied passwords off the clipboard again and checks every save before it replaces your vault. No account, no cloud, no tracking.

Download Free and open source · Other platforms

A vault file you own

Your passwords stay in one file, wherever you keep it: on your disk, a USB stick or a folder you sync yourself. Nobody else holds a copy, and there's no account that can be locked or breached.

The file is a KDBX vault, the open format many password managers read and write, so you can switch apps or use another one on your phone.

  • Strong, modern encryption
  • Works with other KDBX apps
  • No account or sign-in
  • Nothing leaves your computer

Opens the vaults you have

Older vaults open as they are. The first change asks before it updates the file to the current format, and the old file stays beside it as a backup.

  • KDBX 4.1
  • KDBX 4.0
  • KDBX 3.1
  • KDB

Copy a password, and it's gone again

Press Enter or ⌘C to copy the password, ⌘B for the username and ⌘T for the one-time code. After 20 seconds the password comes off the clipboard, unless you've copied something else since.

Copies are marked so clipboard histories leave them out. Passwords show only when you ask, with F3, and hide again after a few seconds.

Strong passwords at a key press

In the entry editor, F2 makes a random password from your computer's secure random source. Choose the length and the kinds of characters, and leave out ones that look alike.

The strength shows as you change it, and your choices are kept for next time.

What sets it apart

Every save is checked

A save goes to a new file first, which is opened again and compared with what you changed. Only then does it replace your vault, and the previous version stays as a backup.

Locks itself

After five minutes without a key press or click, when you quit, or right away with ⌘⇧L. Locking saves first, then wipes the open vault from memory.

One-time codes

Keep a login's two-step codes with its password. The code shows with the seconds it has left, and ⌘T copies it.

A recycle bin

Deleted entries go to the bin, and ⌘Z brings the last one back. F2 shows the bin, where entries go back to the group they came from.

Nothing lost in an edit

Each change keeps the entry's previous version in its history, so an old password is still there when a site didn't take the new one.

Find it as you type

Start typing and the list narrows to matching titles, usernames, addresses and groups. F5 picks the groups to show, so work and home stay apart.

Keys

Every action has a key, and the key bar shows the common ones. Type to search. The mouse works too: click a copy icon in the details to copy that field.

On Windows and Linux, use Ctrl instead of ⌘.
